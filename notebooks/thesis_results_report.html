Headline comparison

RubricNet is strictly additive: each hand-crafted descriptor gets its own tiny subnetwork, the outputs are summed, and an ordinal head turns the sum into a class prediction — no cross-descriptor interactions. That constraint is what makes it interpretable, and the question is what it costs against a black-box model.

Interpretability: descriptor importance & monotonicity

This is the actual contribution of the thesis. Because RubricNet is additive (score = Σi gi(xi), no interaction terms), each descriptor’s learned subnetwork gi can be inspected directly.

Top 5 most influential descriptors (RubricNet V3)

  1. log_total_notes — overall piece length / density
  2. total_notes — overall piece length / density
  3. shift_rate — how often the fretting hand changes position
  4. open_string_ratio — use of open strings (negative weight — more open strings, easier)
  5. string_entropy — right-hand string-crossing variety

These match established classical-guitar pedagogy — position shifts, open-string usage, and right-hand variety are textbook difficulty drivers — which is the qualitative check behind the quantitative importance-alignment plot above.

Fuzzy rule-based baselines

A second, independent interpretable baseline (adapted from Heerde, Vatolkin & Rudolph, EvoMUSART 2020): descriptors are fuzzified into 5 triangular linguistic terms, and classification is either a complete search over primitive rules or a fuzzy pattern tree (FPT).

Considerably weaker than RubricNet on every metric (table in §02), but a useful sanity check: the top rules for the easiest class cite low fret position, small stretches, and stable hand position; the top rules for the hardest class cite the opposite plus high note count — qualitatively the same story RubricNet’s descriptor weights tell.

Summary

  • Headline claim, earned — RubricNet (fully interpretable, additive) beats Random Forest (black-box) on 4/6 metrics, and is well ahead of both fuzzy rule-based interpretable baselines on every metric.
  • Interpretability is the point — monotonic per-descriptor subnetworks plus additive decomposition give a literal, human-readable rubric per prediction, and the descriptors RubricNet leans on match both Random Forest’s feature importances and classical guitar pedagogy.
  • What was tried and didn’t help (documented, excluded from the headline) — hand-crafted interaction/product features hurt all three model families via collinearity with the base descriptors; feature pruning, label smoothing, and a multi-task coarse head were within noise or negative. Full ablation notes in guitar/ABLATION_INTERACTION_FEATURES.md and guitar/RESULTS.md.
